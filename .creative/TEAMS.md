# Teams

A Creative Team contains exactly three specialist roles and is chaired by the
Arbiter. Select the team from the initiative's dominant problem. If the owner
names a team, use it. Otherwise
infer the closest team from the prompt and record the choice in the session.

Each role retains its own assigned GT Concepts from `GT-concepts.md`. The order
below is speaking order for the first pass, not a sequence of competing
positions. The Arbiter synthesizes after every complete pass and invites
another pass when the shared concept needs formation or refinement.

## Catalyst
Roles:
1. Visionary
2. Contrarian
3. Inventor

Use for: broad invention, new concepts, disruptive alternatives, and problems
where the current framing may itself be wrong.

Complementary attention: possibility, fragility, and executable mechanism.

## Experience
Roles:
1. Visionary
2. Player Advocate
3. Artist

Use for: UX, interaction, product feel, presentation, narrative, identity,
onboarding, and emotionally legible experiences.

Complementary attention: possibility, lived experience, and expression.

## Systems
Roles:
1. Systems Thinker
2. Contrarian
3. Player Advocate

Use for: workflows, architecture at the product level, rules, incentives,
complex interaction systems, and changes with significant second-order effects.

Complementary attention: systemic effects, robustness, and human experience.

## Studio
Roles:
1. Artist
2. Inventor
3. Player Advocate

Use for: visual design, physical design, UI styling, layout, components,
industrial design, and turning aesthetic direction into reviewable artifacts.

Complementary attention: expression, realization, and lived experience.

## Emergence
Roles:
1. Visionary
2. Systems Thinker
3. Inventor

Use for: generative systems, novel product mechanics, platforms, tools, and
concepts whose value emerges from interacting parts.

Complementary attention: possibility, interacting structure, and mechanism.

## Crucible
Roles:
1. Contrarian
2. Artist
3. Systems Thinker

Use for: refinement of an existing direction that feels weak, generic,
incoherent, overcomplicated, or insufficiently differentiated.

Complementary attention: hidden weaknesses, expressive fit, and systemic
coherence of an existing direction.
